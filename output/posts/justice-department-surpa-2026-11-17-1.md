## 어떤 발표인가

- RECA was originally enacted in 1990 as a non-adversarial alternative to litigation for individuals who contracted certain illnesses following exposure to radiation as a result of the United States’ atmospheric nuclear testing program and uranium ore processing operations during the Cold War.
- The law sunset in 2024 and the Department ceased accepting new claims.
- In 2025, as part of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, Congress reauthorized the law and expanded the scope of the RECA’s coverage.
- “We are proud that the Civil Division has delivered more than $1 billion in compensation to Americans under the reauthorized Radiation Exposure Compensation Act.
- Congress determined that these individuals deserve compensation, and the Civil Division is honored to help ensure they receive the benefits that Congress provided,” said Acting Assistant Attorney General Jonathan Guynn of the Justice Department’s Civil Division.
