미 연방거래위원회가(Federal Trade Commission) 2026-10-22 공개한 'FTC Sues Perpetrators of Government Impersonation Scheme Over Deceptive Ads, Misleading Promises and Fake Reviews' 발표를 바탕으로, 확인 가능한 사실을 중심으로 정리했습니다.

## 핵심 내용과 국내 파급력

The Federal Trade Commission is taking action against GovAssist LLC, Entry LLC and Scott Zuckerman , for their alleged involvement in a government impersonation scheme that deceived consumers seeking passport services by purporting to be the federal government or affiliated with the federal government. 소비자 보호나 개인정보, 결제 관행과 관련된 발표라면 국내 서비스 이용자도 유사한 약관, 환불, 데이터 활용 방식을 비교해 볼 수 있습니다. 특히 플랫폼 기업이나 해외 서비스를 쓰는 경우에는 미국 규제기관이 문제 삼은 행위가 무엇인지 확인하는 것이 도움이 됩니다.

## 어떤 발표인가

- The FTC alleges the operation uses deceptive paid search ads to impersonate the government or pretend to be affiliated with the government to lure travelers seeking passport services to defendants’ websites and makes false service promises to obtain consumers’ financial and personal information.
- “Using paid Google search ads and landing pages to impersonate the government and target American travelers is illegal,” said Christopher Mufarrige, Director of the FTC’s Bureau of Consumer Protection.
- “Today’s action should serve as a stark warning that the FTC will pursue all bad actors participating in search advertising schemes that mislead consumers, distort competition, or otherwise violate the FTC Act.” Since at least October 2023, GovAssist, Entry and Zuckerman have allegedly hosted a series of websites including GovAssist.com, Entry.com and IVS.us to offer passports and passport-related services, as well as other travel-related services such as visa applications and visa renewals, while pretending to be affiliated with the U.S.
- The complaint alleges the defendants violate the law by: Illegally impersonating the government.
- The defendants use paid Google search ads to position their websites where consumers would expect to find and access official federal government websites and services.
