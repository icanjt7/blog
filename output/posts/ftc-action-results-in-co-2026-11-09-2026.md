미 연방거래위원회가(Federal Trade Commission) 2026-11-09 공개한 'FTC Action Results in Court Issuing Temporary Restraining Order Halting Phantom Debt Collection Operation' 발표를 바탕으로, 확인 가능한 사실을 중심으로 정리했습니다.

## 핵심 내용과 국내 파급력

At the Federal Trade Commission’s request, a federal court has temporarily halted and frozen the assets of a debt collection operation that allegedly used deceptive and unlawful tactics to pressure consumers into paying debts that they frequently did not owe. 소비자 보호나 개인정보, 결제 관행과 관련된 발표라면 국내 서비스 이용자도 유사한 약관, 환불, 데이터 활용 방식을 비교해 볼 수 있습니다. 특히 플랫폼 기업이나 해외 서비스를 쓰는 경우에는 미국 규제기관이 문제 삼은 행위가 무엇인지 확인하는 것이 도움이 됩니다.

## 어떤 발표인가

- “Pretending to be a law firm or affiliated with attorneys and threatening to contact consumers’ employers and family members to induce consumers to pay debts they do not owe is illegal,” said Christopher Mufarriage, Director of the FTC’s Bureau of Consumer Protection.
- “We are pleased that the court stopped this scheme—sending a clear message that the FTC will not tolerate bad actors using unlawful debt collection tactics to coerce consumers into paying.” According to the complaint, McDonnell & Associates LLC, also doing business as First Capital Group , Maxwell & Bryant Group and Lewis, McDonnell & Associate LLC and owners, Oscar Gerardo Gonzalez Martin and Oscar Ruiz Canela, falsely claimed to be a law firm or affiliated with attorneys and used high-pressure tactics to coerce consumers to make payments on debts that the consumers often did not owe, a practice known as phantom debt collection.
- For instance, the complaint alleges that the defendants left deceptive voicemail messages to get consumers to respond and threatened to contact consumers’ employers and family members if they did not.
- The complaint further alleges that when consumers did call back, the defendants would make a series of false representations and threats designed to coerce payment from consumers.
- For example, the defendants threatened to take legal action against consumers if they did not pay the alleged debt, garnish consumers’ wages and put a lien on their properties.
